# Architecture Position Defined

## Overview

An architecture position is a detailed point of view on a complex topic. Positions articulate when to use or when not to use certain technologies or approaches.

## Executive Summary

### Statement of Position

- Clearly and concisely state the position in a single sentence or two. Follow with one or two more paragraphs of explanation if necessary. If the position represents a specific decision, write with finality and be explicit about decision scope (technological, organizational, geographical, temporal, etc.). If the position represents a point of view, be clear about critical assumptions.

### Basis

- Use this space to summarize the basis for the position. What is the rationale? What is driving the need to take a position? What other options were considered and why is this position taken within the stated scope? What inputs were considered in taking the position (e.g. analysts, industry groups, Entegris-specific requirements)?

### Implications

- Use this space to summarize the implications of taking this position. What would need to change? What architecture areas may be impacted?

## Position Detail (Optional)

Positions that require more robust support and/or detailed explanation may choose to use the following sections:

### Background / Motivation

- Provide a more detailed account of the history and motivation for the position. This contextual information can often help in understanding and interpreting the applicability of positions to future situations.

### Subject Area Overview

- Often times Enterprise Architecture is asked to take a position on emerging technology, or sort through a particularly complex problem space. In those cases it is often necessary to elaborate on history, definitions, industry trends, and other baseline facts relevant to the position.

### Detailed Statement of Position

- Begin this section with a restatement of the statement of position from the executive summary, followed by more a more detailed or nuanced version if necessary.

### Options / Variants

- Since a position is by definition a choice among options, this section should elaborate on the alternatives to the stated position and why they were not selected.

### Detailed Basis

- Begin this section with a restatement of the basis from the executive summary, followed by more a more detailed version if necessary (e.g. sub-sections for each logical component of the rationale).

### Assumptions

- List the assumptions on which the position is based. Assumptions can be very important in determining the applicability of the position to a specific situation in the future.

### Detailed Implications

- Begin this section with a restatement of the implications from the executive summary, followed by more a more detailed version if necessary (e.g. sub-sections for each impact area).

### Feasibility / Scalability

- Related to implications, this section should contain more quantitative analysis of the impact of the position and what will be required for implementation.
