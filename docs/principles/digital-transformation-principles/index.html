Digital Transformation (DX) Principles

DX is defined as creating business value through the use of digital tools, technologies, and solutions. Digital = Physical, Virtual, and Data.

Create Differentiated Customer Value​

Principle Name: Create Differentiated Customer Value

Statement: Digital investments and initiatives are centered around creating differentiated, measurable value for Entegris customers — not internal efficiency alone.

Rationale: Entegris's 2030 vision is to become its customers' most dependable electronic materials platform. Digital transformation only advances that vision when it produces outcomes customers experience.

Implications:

  • Digital investments should be evaluated primarily by customer value impact, not internal cost metrics.
  • Customer outcomes should be explicitly defined in digital initiative charters.
  • Portfolio prioritization should favor programs that create measurable differentiated customer value.

Focus on Business Outcomes, Not Tools​

Principle Name: Focus on Business Outcomes, Not Tools

Statement: Digital initiatives must begin with a defined, measurable business outcome. Technology selection follows outcome definition — never the reverse.

Rationale: Tool-first delivery creates solutions looking for problems. Outcome-first delivery ensures every technology investment is tied to a business result that can be measured and tracked.

Implications:

  • Use cases must define measurable success criteria and KPIs before technology selection begins.
  • KPI design should track business outcome realization, not feature delivery or go-live dates.
  • Tool choices should follow business objective clarity, not vendor preference or trend.

Iterative Launch and Learn​

Principle Name: Iterative Launch and Learn

Statement: Digital delivery should use an iterative, evidence-based Launch and Learn approach — releasing early, measuring outcomes, and using findings to drive the next iteration.

Rationale: Evidence-based iteration reduces delivery risk, accelerates time to value, and prevents large investments in unvalidated assumptions.

Implications:

  • Delivery cycles should be short enough to produce measurable learning within weeks, not quarters.
  • Early releases should be instrumented to capture the metrics defined in the business case.
  • Findings from each iteration should directly drive backlog prioritization and roadmap adjustment.

End-to-End Integrated Experience​

Principle Name: End-to-End Integrated Experience

Statement: Digital solutions should be designed with an end-to-end mindset, ensuring connected experiences that eliminate fragmentation across process, data, and system boundaries.

Rationale: Siloed digital solutions create fragmented customer and employee experiences, hidden integration costs, and barriers to enterprise-wide analytics and insight.

Implications:

  • Solution design should explicitly consider upstream and downstream integration points.
  • Data and process handoffs should be designed for continuity, not treated as edge cases.
  • Cross-functional teams should coordinate to avoid independently optimized silos.

Fast Follower on Exponential Technology​

Principle Name: Fast Follower on Exponential Technology

Statement: Entegris adopts proven exponential technologies as a disciplined fast follower — moving quickly once enterprise value is demonstrated, rather than pioneering unproven technology at scale.

Rationale: First-mover adoption of emerging technology carries high risk and distraction cost. Fast-follower adoption captures the value without the experimentation overhead, aligned to Entegris's pragmatic, value-driven technology posture.

Implications:

  • Technology scanning should focus on identifying proven enterprise value patterns in adjacent industries.
  • Adoption decisions should weigh time-to-value, implementation risk, and organizational readiness.
  • Pilots should validate fit for Entegris's specific context before scaled rollout is approved.

Data as an Asset for Actionable Insights​

Principle Name: Data as an Asset for Actionable Insights

Statement: Treat accurate and timely data as a strategic asset that unlocks valuable, actionable insights — and design digital solutions to generate and surface those insights by default.

Rationale: The ability to derive insight from operational data is a core source of competitive advantage in manufacturing and materials science. Digital solutions that do not produce usable data create blind spots.

Implications:

  • Data quality and timeliness should be managed as first-class strategic priorities, not infrastructure afterthoughts.
  • Insight generation should be designed into digital solutions from the start, not added post-launch.
  • Governance should support trusted, accessible analytical outcomes for decision makers.

Customer Digital Experience Principles

Simple​

Principle Name: Simple

Statement: Customer digital experiences should be designed for speed, effortlessness, and effectiveness — minimizing the steps, decisions, and effort required to accomplish any goal.

Rationale: Customers engaging with Entegris digitally are technical professionals with high expectations and limited patience for complex interfaces. Simple experiences drive adoption, reduce errors, and lower support costs.

Implications:

  • Customer journeys should be mapped and tested end-to-end for clarity and ease before release.
  • Interfaces should eliminate unnecessary steps, decisions, and data entry wherever possible.
  • Onboarding should be minimized — customers should be able to self-serve without extensive training.

Engaging​

Principle Name: Engaging

Statement: Customer digital experiences should be designed for relevance and value — presenting information and functionality that is contextually useful, not generically complete.

Rationale: Relevant, personalized experiences create stronger customer engagement, increase return usage, and differentiate Entegris from competitors offering generic portals.

Implications:

  • Content and functionality should be curated based on the customer's product line, role, and history.
  • Experiences should be measured for engagement and continuously improved based on usage data.
  • Irrelevant or unused features should be de-emphasized or removed to reduce noise.

Transparent​

Principle Name: Transparent

Statement: Customer digital experiences should build trust by proactively providing the data, status, terms, and context customers need to make informed decisions — without requiring them to ask.

Rationale: Transparency is a key driver of customer trust and satisfaction in B2B relationships. Customers who can see order status, quality data, and material traceability in real time are less likely to escalate issues and more likely to deepen their relationship with Entegris.

Implications:

  • Order status, shipment tracking, and quality event data should be surfaced proactively, not reactively.
  • Customer-facing decisions (pricing, availability, specifications) should be explainable and verifiable.
  • Communication around issues, delays, or changes should be honest, timely, and specific.

Collaborative​

Principle Name: Collaborative

Statement: Customer digital experiences should enable open collaboration — allowing customers and Entegris teams to connect, communicate, share data, and transact within a secure, structured framework.

Rationale: Deep collaboration with key customers is a source of innovation, loyalty, and competitive differentiation. Digital tools that facilitate that collaboration amplify the value of the relationship.

Implications:

  • Collaboration features (data sharing, sample tracking, joint development workflows) should be designed with security and access control as first-class requirements.
  • Customer feedback mechanisms should be embedded in the experience, not bolted on afterward.
  • Interaction patterns should support async and synchronous collaboration to fit customer workflows.

Customer-Centric​

Principle Name: Customer-Centric

Statement: Every decision in customer digital experience design should prioritize the customer's needs, workflows, and outcomes — not internal convenience or legacy system constraints.

Rationale: Customer-centric design produces stronger adoption, higher satisfaction, and competitive differentiation. Internal constraints should be resolved internally, not exposed to the customer as their problem to work around.

Implications:

  • Customer impact should be the primary evaluation criterion for digital experience design decisions.
  • Customer feedback and usage data should directly influence roadmap and design priorities.
  • Support and service design should be aligned to the customer journey, not the organizational structure.
